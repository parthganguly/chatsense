**Strongest competing explanation.** A product-relevant reading might interpret correct D historical arithmetic followed by “Yes, 70%” as evidence that users mistake past frequency for a calibrated forecast, or interpret E overclaims as users confusing insufficient evidence with no contact. But this pilot observes **service × request × item**, not people. The strongest alternative is **forced-choice output construction**: provider-specific language-model policies combine pretrained narrative completion, numerical and lexical lures, option-length/complexity cues, decoding, and—in image conditions—input processing. Thus 7/10 can directly prime “70%,” while “no calibrated probability” requires a less familiar metalinguistic judgment. On E, sparse-chat narratives can supply plausible but unwarranted explanations. Conversely, an always-uncertain or longest-option heuristic can mimic comprehension on c-referenced scope items. Neither errors nor all-reference performance requires any corresponding human misunderstanding.

**Prediction the fixed data could contradict.** A concrete output-policy version predicts an **isolated numerical-lure dissociation**: D q04 selects “Yes, 70%” while q01–q03 and q05 remain correct, because q05 lacks the direct 7/10→70% lure. It also predicts that simple uncertainty/option-length behavior reproduces some apparently sophisticated E scope selections without consistently correct factual extraction. The fixed results could contradict this concrete version by showing stable, broader overclaiming across D q04–q06, rather than an isolated q04 error, alongside scope performance that tracks correct extraction and is not reproduced by the comparator. That would favor a different model-interpretation account—not establish a human one. A repeat differences comparable to image/text differences would likewise leave modality explanations unresolved.

**Missing comparison.** The frozen design lacks semantically equivalent items with option verbosity, epistemic wording, key/order placement, and numerical lures independently counterbalanced; D/E also lack repeat-noise estimates. These are limitations, not authorization to extend the schedule. Most importantly, there is no target-user comparison with open-ended explanations or behavior beyond the same susceptible multiple-choice format.

**Evidence needed before a product decision.** Separately authorized work should first test whether model patterns survive paraphrase and counterbalancing and repeat across runs. Surface-sensitive reversals would support the instrument/output-policy alternative. Human evidence would require representative users, preregistered comprehension measures, explanations demonstrating the alleged inference, and ideally a product-relevant behavioral consequence. Agreement between model argmax and human answers alone would remain insufficient.

**Actionable diagnostic lead.** A validated, specific pattern can justify offline engineering investigation: for example, repeated A image failure with text recovery warrants inspection of the image-input pathway, without isolating OCR or layout. Correct D arithmetic plus q04 error similarly motivates testing numerical-lure sensitivity. Such leads can prioritize debugging or future controlled studies, but not UX changes, prevalence claims, or release decisions.
