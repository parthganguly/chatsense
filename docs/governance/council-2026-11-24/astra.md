CHATSENSE-ASTRA-24 — Frontier scientist, independent first pass

**No outcome of the 16 requests would, by itself, justify changing a ChatSense card.** The experiment can establish which interpretations Jev and GLiDE favor when given a fixed card, a question, and specified alternatives. Its strongest scientific use is to rule out narrow explanations for particular answer patterns. No experiment data exist. At this rehearsal, there are 0/16 validated requests, 0/128 item distributions, 0/8 available modality contrasts, and 0/4 available repeat contrasts. B and C have no planned observations.

**The human problem may be what to do with a fact one understands.** A person could correctly read that Ravi restarted seven of ten earlier quiet stretches, recognize that the card gives no calibrated forecast, and still ask, “Should I wait for him to write?” Waiting could reflect a preference about who initiates contact, even if the reader assigns no predictive weight to 7/10. Conversely, someone could select the reference answer because “calibrated” sounds too strong while privately treating 70% as a forecast. The same prompted answer is compatible with different beliefs, and the same decision is compatible with different readings. My hypothesis is that a reader may understand the statistic and still be uncertain how to act. The pilot observes neither that uncertainty nor the decision.

**Three item patterns would discriminate among narrower accounts.**

- **A, extraction versus interpretation.** Suppose both image rounds strongly favor “5 minutes then 1.5 hours” on q01, yet favor a nonreference direction on q02 or q03. That would falsify the claim that every direction disagreement coincides with a failure to select the displayed values. It would leave open whether the service misread the relation or mapped it to the wrong answer. If the same direction item recovers in text in both rounds, compare its signed option shifts and image/text total variation with its own within-modality repeat variation. A recurring shift would support an input-format hypothesis. OCR, layout, and question interpretation could still produce the same pattern. A shift comparable to repeat variation leaves the modality explanation unresolved.
- **D, history versus prediction.** Strong preference for Ravi on q02 and 7/10 on q03 alongside “Yes, 70%” on q04 would defeat the claim that a forecast disagreement requires a wrong historical fraction. Copying the salient number into the forecast answer and interpreting the card as endorsing a forecast are observationally equivalent here. D q05 distinguishes the separate claim that the current quiet is guaranteed to end. D has no repeat to establish stability.
- **E, zero of what?** Selecting one conversation start on q02, zero eligible windows on q03, and the four-window requirement on q04, then selecting “No other contact exists” on q05, would defeat the account that the outside-contact answer requires mistaking zero windows for zero starts. It would locate the disagreement at the boundary of export coverage. A single E response cannot establish why it arose or whether it recurs.

These are predictions about observable selections. Each item yields an option distribution, and the eight distributions in a request do not trace a single respondent’s reasoning. An all-reference result could arise from accurate reading or from extracting numbers and favoring cautious wording elsewhere. The always-uncertain comparator helps expose the latter possibility. Fixed option order and bundled questions leave cue use unresolved. Provider differences also admit a timing explanation, particularly with an unpinned GLiDE service.

**The strongest product-relevant outcome would be a specific question for later review.** For example, the D pattern could motivate asking whether readers carry a historical fraction into a forecast. A defensible copy hypothesis would quote the exact card and item, show all option probabilities and ties by service and condition, and retain the salient-number explanation. A validated response can document a disagreement even if its mate is missing. Only a complete pair supports a contrast. Neither repeated disagreement nor unanimous agreement measures human comprehension, harm, or the benefit of an edit. A product change would need independent evidence that the wording misleads intended readers and that a proposed change addresses that difficulty. This read-only rehearsal authorizes no execution or product action.
