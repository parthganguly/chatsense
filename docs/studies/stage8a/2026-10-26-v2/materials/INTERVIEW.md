# Interview guide — verbatim, approximately 30 minutes

Facilitator-only preparation: approved consent and P1 authorization, private form, participant code/order, exact selected screenshot hashes, native-scale display that fits the whole card, timer. No personal exports; no audio/video. Record consent privately; no card before consent. The participant must never see CODING-RUBRIC.md, expected answers, fake controls or gate thresholds.

Opening:
"Thank you. We are evaluating how these fictional screens come across. Please say what you think, even if something is unclear or unhelpful. You can skip any question, take a break or stop. Please do not share messages or identifying details about your own conversations. I will wait until the end to explain the screens so that I can first hear your interpretation."

For each card in the assigned cyclic order:
- B context only: "For this example, imagine a chat between two friends."
- A/C/D/E: "This is another fictional chat."
- "Please read this silently for ten seconds. After that I will ask you some questions. You may keep looking at it while answering."

Show entire selected screenshot; time ten seconds; record actual time and interruptions. Do not explain the confidence tag, evidence, history, caveat or relationship type. Do not call D a prediction test or E the correct answer.

1. "What does this tell you?"
2. "What does it not tell you?"
3. "Will they reply, according to this?"
4. "How useful is this screen to you, from 1, not at all useful, to 5, extremely useful? Two means slightly useful, three moderately useful, and four very useful. You can also choose not to answer."
5. "How creepy or intrusive does this screen feel, from 1, not at all, to 5, extremely? Two means slightly, three moderately, and four very. You can also choose not to answer."
6. "Would you want this about a chat of yours?" Record yes/no/unsure/prefer not and the exact response.
7. D ONLY, after all preceding questions: "What do you expect to happen next in that chat?"

Use silence and a neutral "Thank you" between answers; no praise for a correct interpretation. A person may spontaneously revise an answer; keep the first wording and revision in chronological order. The initial misunderstanding remains in coding.

Allowed neutral probe, only if meaning cannot be recorded: "Could you say more about what you mean?" One probe per ambiguous answer; log exact wording and response. If attribution of an expectation remains unclear after the prescribed D question, use at most once: "What are you basing that expectation on?" Ask after the initial response, never suggest "history", "prediction", "motives" or the correct answer. No forced yes/no "understanding" choice. If asked for interpretation, say "I would like to hear your interpretation first; we can discuss it at the end." If asked to repeat the question, repeat verbatim and log it. Do not ask missing motive concepts directly to improve the primary score.

After all five cards, in this order:
1. "Which card would you screenshot for a friend, if any?"
2. "Would you check the same chat again in a few weeks?"
3. "Overall, how much do you trust what you saw, from 1, no trust, to 5, very high trust? Two is low, three moderate and four high. You may choose not to answer."
4. "Was the evidence visible enough or too buried?"

These are research questions, not invitations to upload a real chat or set reminders. Ask no extra persuasion questions. If needed, show the five identifiers/thumbnails without changing any prior answers.

Debrief AFTER all response fields are closed:
"These fictional examples describe counts and timing in an export. They do not establish why anyone acted that way or what they will do next. A history of a chat restarting is not a promise of a future reply. Thank you for helping us understand how the screens communicate. Your study code is [code]. You can contact [approved contact] within fourteen days to withdraw your notes. You do not need to try this with your own messages."

Record end time, skips, visibility/order/timing deviations, probes, corrections/coaching and any personal-content redaction incident without the content. A facilitator never supplies the coding verdict in the participant's form.
