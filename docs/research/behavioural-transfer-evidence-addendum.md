## Decision delta

**Change the evidence characterization, not the product decision.** There is now directly reviewed WhatsApp/Instagram messaging-metadata evidence for similarity of partners' response-time distributions in selected sustained dyads. It strengthens the rationale for studying dyadic timing and the need to control partner/context effects. It does **not** demonstrate that a person's habits predict their behavior in an unseen relationship, that the same person's behavior transfers across media, or that metadata measures personality.

## S25 — Direct messaging metadata, with a limited target

**Martin, Hakobyan & Drimalla (2026), _Sorry for the late reply: Response times and reciprocity in WhatsApp and Instagram chats_.** [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03687), [v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03687v1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.03687). Treat the inspected May 5 v1 as a **preprint**; the inspected record supplies no established journal publication. Original methods/results/appendices and public analysis source were inspected by lead/Sol, without accessing participant data or running the notebooks.

**Population and selection.** Of 129 WhatsApp and 66 Instagram donations, 64 and 33 remained: 97 donations, 889 chats (344/545), and 3,423,575 messages before same-sender block merging (Table 1). No participant donated both platforms. Responding donors were predominantly young (age median 24, N=93); partner demographics were unavailable. WhatsApp donors selected their 5–7 most important chats. Instagram could supply all chats, with a default one-year export window and different media coverage; mean donated spans were 1,630 versus 357 days. The paper pools data from multiple studies, rather than providing multiple independent replications.

Eligibility removed chats below 100 messages, chats with either sender below 10% of messages, and donors with fewer than five remaining chats. Thus the study conditions on sustained two-way participation and on having enough qualifying contacts. About half of original donations remain (97/195); this is a descriptive retention calculation, not a forecast of F1 recruitment. Very unequal, sparse, stopped or low-contact relationships are underrepresented by design. Appendix A also describes corrections to Instagram donor identification in 20 donations. These are selected records, not a representative communication census or 889 independent people.

**Measures and findings.** Same-sender consecutive messages are merged; latency runs from the last message in the preceding block to the first in the responding block. Seven minute bins define each sender's distribution. Similarity is one minus base-2 Jensen–Shannon distance. No independent personality inventory, perceived reciprocity or chat-specific closeness criterion validates this interpretation.

| Estimand | Reported result | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Pooled observed delays at most five minutes | WhatsApp 68.88%; Instagram 43.62% (§4.1); uncertainty intervals not reported there | Neither a probability of answering an arbitrary message nor a controlled platform effect; unanswered terminal messages are not observed switches. |
| Per-chat distribution similarity | WhatsApp mean .88, SD .07; Instagram .76, SD .10 (§4.2); SD is not a CI | Similarity is not affection, closeness, actor reliability or calibrated confidence. |
| Donor-intercept mixed model: ego fast-response fraction regressed on partner fraction | Table 2: WhatsApp slope .786, SE .024, 95% CI [.739,.834]; Instagram .796, SE .020, CI [.758,.834] | These are unstandardized contemporaneous association slopes, not correlations, predictive accuracy, causal adaptation or held-out transfer. |
| Monthly persistence of dyadic similarity | Reported per-chat median absolute deviation .01–.07; Appendix B says monthly eligibility reduces 889 chats to 180 | A selected same-dyad descriptive stability result, not an actor ICC, a test of new partners, or proof that either person's latency stayed stable. |

The mixed model accounts for repeated chats from a donor using a random intercept. It does not identify separate actor, partner and relationship causes, establish unique partners across donations, or independently control all shared schedules. Its slope mixes within-donor and between-donor information unless partner fractions are explicitly decomposed into donor means and deviations. Random slopes/time controls were tried and omitted after model comparison (Appendix D); absence of improvement is not proof that those factors never matter. No untouched person, future-period or platform test is reported for the central regression.

**A falsifying counterexample to the inference, not to the observation:** in month one, both partners' delays all occupy a fast bin; in month two, both occupy a slow bin. Each month's partner distributions are identical, so similarity remains 1 and its monthly deviation remains 0, despite a large change in both people's latency. Stable balance therefore cannot substitute for H1's future latency prediction. Also, two delays of 61 minutes and a year occupy the same >60-minute bin; similarity cannot bound their absolute difference. A prevalence-aware reference is needed: two independently sampled fast-response distributions may resemble each other without partner-specific matching. Similarly, equal counts can occur in long one-sided runs or alternating exchanges: volume balance and temporal reciprocity are different targets (S27).

### Reproducibility qualifications and ChatSense definitions

Public code inspected at [`10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177`](https://github.com/mbp-lab/dona-rt2026/tree/10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177). Sol found the following source/text mismatches; their effect on published estimates is **unquantified** because no study data or notebooks were run:

- Table 2's caption says one minute, while §§3.5/4.2 and the notebook specify five minutes. Use the table's .786/.796 slopes with that qualification; nearby prose says .787/.797.
- §3.3 says round delays **up** to minutes; [`src/etl.py` lines 553–568](https://github.com/mbp-lab/dona-rt2026/blob/10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177/src/etl.py#L553) uses nearest-minute rounding and a minimum of one. A 5m20s delay can therefore classify differently at the five-minute cutoff.
- The monthly description specifies at least five eligible months. The [inspected notebook at line 1558](https://github.com/mbp-lab/dona-rt2026/blob/10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177/run_analyses.ipynb#L1558) uses a strict `> 5` filter, requiring six.
- Monthly distributions use right-inclusive `pd.cut` bins ([notebook line 1505](https://github.com/mbp-lab/dona-rt2026/blob/10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177/run_analyses.ipynb#L1505)); Appendix B's sampling-noise procedure uses `np.histogram` ([line 1900](https://github.com/mbp-lab/dona-rt2026/blob/10eb89579e6fafebc2728d6c5f51abb5c12f0177/run_analyses.ipynb#L1900)) with the same edges but different edge inclusion. Exact-minute values can enter different bins. That matters especially after minute rounding; the noise threshold is not a directly reusable reliability certificate.

ChatSense's pinned core (`2415a51`) computes adjacent different-sender delays with exact nonnegative elapsed minutes in `buildReplyEvents`/`gapMinutes`. Its switch boundaries are comparable to last-to-first block boundaries, but it does not apply the preprint's minimum-one-minute rounding or donor/chat filters. Its turns additionally split at six hours; reply events are computed separately and are not automatically restricted to a six-hour thread. Neither definition can establish that the later message semantically answers the earlier one. Preserve long-gap/restart sensitivity and terminal censoring; do not copy the paper's fast percentages, bins, similarity score or minimum counts into the product.

The study locally reduces content and then submits derived metadata to a backend. That is **not** ChatSense's local-only processing promise. The authors' anonymization terminology does not establish absence of reidentification risk or authorize uploads here.

## Consequences for the proposed experiment

These are amendments for **future preregistration**, not results or an expanded implementation task. The [protocol's amendment section](validation-protocol.md#7-bounded-evidence-amendment-2026-10-11) is authoritative for integrating them.

1. **Keep strict H1 intact:** use other-dyad weeks 1–4 to predict target-dyad weeks 5–8, with no target-dyad history supplied. Neither the partner's concurrent test-period latency nor the focal person's target history is an admissible predictor. Success establishes conditional predictive transfer in selected relationships, not a causal actor trait.
2. **Make the competing target explicit:** in the separately declared same-dyad task, compare context + lagged partner timing against that model + focal person's other-dyad history; also retain the focal-dyad historical baseline. This can test incremental value when target history exists, but must never be reported as unseen-dyad success. If personal history adds nothing, its utility when target history exists is limited; this does not negate a positive strict-H1 result when target history is unavailable. A truly isolated actor-versus-partner effect needs a connected crossed design and separate consent/resources.
3. **Do not screen for the desired conclusion:** retain unequal relationships when otherwise eligible; no new 10% participation cutoff or “most important chats only” requirement. Report all donor/dyad/period exclusions and eligibility by prior relationship context and voluntary closeness reports, if collected independently. Freeze the three dyad IDs at baseline under a baseline-only selection rule among mutually consenting candidates; assess future eligibility without replacing dyads using weeks 5–8 activity. Distinguish that rule from convenience donation; neither samples the unobserved network. Check whether apparent transfer survives dropping the most active/closest eligible dyad.
4. **Test stability of the actual outcome:** distributional similarity, actor conditional median and count reciprocity are separate estimands. Keep the proposed 20-switch floor provisional. Assess median uncertainty at 10/20/40 switches, time-block sensitivity and synchronized actor/partner changes. S25's monthly 70-message calibration cannot validate this floor.
5. **Freeze temporal definitions and falsifiers:** minute rounding, bin closure, block boundaries, completed gaps and study-window coverage need explicit synthetic boundary checks before data collection. A common-schedule/partner-response process without stable actor parameters should be included in the future simulation suite. If it produces the proposed actor interpretation or inflated nominal error rates, revise the interpretation/design rather than declare a trait. No simulation was run here.

There is still no reviewed study here demonstrating the exact H1 outcome on untouched people and future dyads, nor paired-person WhatsApp-to-Instagram generalization. That is a bounded search result, not a claim that such literature cannot exist. Evidence now better motivates the study's **alternatives and failure modes**; it does not supply expected H1 effect size, F1 eligibility rate, or F2 power parameters.
